Lucienne Jewelry is one name, one bench, and a set of tools that have not changed much in a hundred years. I trained as a goldsmith in Providence, spent four years finishing pieces that carried other people's names on the tag, then opened this studio in 2014 to make work under my own.

Every ring, cuff, and chain that leaves here passes through my hands from sketch to final polish. I still carve most models in wax before they ever touch metal. It takes longer than pulling a shape from a catalog. That is the whole point of doing it this way.

The studio sits on the ground floor of an old foundry building on the east side. Most days you can hear the torch running before you see the sign. I keep the door open when the weather allows it, and I mean it when I say visitors are welcome to watch the work in progress.

The Process

6 stages, bench to box

01

Sourcing

Recycled 14k gold, sterling silver, and stones bought from two dealers I have worked with since 2015. No mystery supply chains.

02

Wax Carving

Each model is carved by hand from a block of jeweler's wax, checked against calipers and a paper sketch pinned above the bench.

03

Casting

The wax is cast in metal at a small foundry six blocks from the studio, run by a caster I have used for nine years running.

04

Setting

Stones are set under a loupe, one prong at a time. A crooked stone gets pulled and reset before it ever reaches a customer.

05

Finishing

Filed, sanded through four grits, and polished by hand. Matte finishes are hand-brushed, never sandblasted.

06

Quality Check

Weighed, measured, and inspected against the original spec before it is boxed. Nothing ships that I would not wear myself.

Visit

Come see the bench where it happens.

The studio is open to visitors most weekdays. Bring a ring you love and I can size or repair it while you wait, or sit down and we'll talk through a custom piece from the first sketch.
